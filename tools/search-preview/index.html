Google search preview

Type a title and description, or load them from a page, and see roughly how they'll look in Google before you publish.

    Desktop

    Mobile

    An estimate: Google trims by pixel width, and sometimes rewrites titles and descriptions.

    How to use it

    1. Type the title and meta description you plan to use, or enter a page's address and load them.
    2. Watch the counters and warnings. Google cuts titles off at roughly 600 pixels on desktop, so the tool measures width, not just characters.
    3. Edit until the important words show up early and nothing gets cut off, then copy your final text back into your site.

    What makes a good title and description

    • Title: what you do, where, and your business name, with the most important words first. For example, "Emergency Plumber in Cherry Hill, NJ | Smith Plumbing".
    • Description: one or two sentences that give a searcher a reason to pick you, such as same-day service, upfront prices or the areas you cover.
    • Give every page its own title and description. Copies of the same text make pages compete with each other.

    Common questions

    How long should a page title be?

    Aim for about 50 to 60 characters. Google cuts titles off by width, roughly 600 pixels on desktop, so wide letters like W and M use up the space faster than narrow ones like i and l.

    How long should a meta description be?

    About 120 to 160 characters. Google often trims longer ones, and phones may show less.

    Why does Google show a different title or description than mine?

    Google sometimes rewrites them when it thinks other text on the page matches a search better. Clear, accurate titles and descriptions that match the page get rewritten less often.

    Does the meta description affect rankings?

    Not directly. It's what persuades people to click, so a clear, specific description can bring you more visitors from the same position.

    Is this preview exact?

    No, it's a close estimate. Google's layout changes over time and varies by device and search, so treat the warnings as a guide.
