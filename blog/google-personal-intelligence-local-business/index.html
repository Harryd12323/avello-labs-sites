Google's Personal Intelligence expands: what local businesses should know

Google has widened what its Personal Intelligence feature can draw on, so answers in Search are becoming more personal. Here is what Google Personal Intelligence means for a local business website.

News · By Harris, Avello Labs · November 24, 2026 · 3 min read

In short

  • Google is letting people connect more of their own information, like Wallet, Drive files and Gemini chats, to its AI answers.
  • Answers in AI Overviews and AI Mode are also starting to reflect a person's recent searches.
  • Two people asking the same question may now get different answers.
  • Your site needs to be clear and easy for Google to understand. You don't need to rebuild anything.

What happened

Google has expanded a feature called Personal Intelligence. It lets a person opt in to connect their own apps to Google's AI answers, so the answers can use their personal context. Search Engine Journal reports that Google Wallet can now be connected in AI Mode, and that rollout has started. Google Drive and the Gemini app are due to follow in the coming weeks.

AI Mode is Google's conversation-style search. AI Overviews are the written summaries at the top of some results. Google also says both will give more personally relevant responses based on what someone searched recently.

Google says people will soon be able to see what personal context was used for an answer and correct it. It is opt-in, and people can switch it off.

The article's author calls this one of the most consequential changes for SEO (search engine optimization, the work of helping your site show up in search). That is his opinion, but it is worth taking seriously.

What it means for a local business

Search used to feel the same for everyone. Now the answer someone gets can depend on who they are and what they have been doing.

Say a homeowner in Camden County has been reading about furnace repair for a few days. Later they ask Google who to call about a cold house. The answer they see may lean on that earlier research. A neighbor asking the same question may see something different.

For you, that makes one thing less useful: checking your own position by searching your business name or main service. What you see may not be what your customers see.

What stays the same is that Google still needs good information about you to include you. That means a clear site, accurate services, your service area, your hours and a Google Business Profile that matches. We don't know exactly how Google will weigh any of this, so we won't guess. Clear, accurate information is the safest thing to have in place.

What to do about it

  1. Read your own homepage and service pages as a stranger would. Does each page say what you do, where you do it and how to reach you?
  2. Make sure your name, address, phone number and hours are the same on your site and your Google Business Profile.
  3. Write down the questions customers really ask you, and answer them on your site in plain sentences. AI answers draw on pages like that. Our guide to AEO (getting recommended by AI tools) covers this.
  4. Check that your site doesn't block the tools that read it. Our AI crawler check does this in a minute.
  5. Don't panic or rebuild. Fix the basics and keep your site up to date. Our free site check is a quick way to see where you stand.
