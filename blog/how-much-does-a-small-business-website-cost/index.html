How much does a small business website cost?

How much does a small business website cost? It depends on a handful of things you can understand before you talk to anyone, and we'll walk through them here.

By Avello Labs · October 28, 2026 · 7 min read

In short

  • The price comes down to how much custom work is needed, how many pages you have, what the site has to do, and who writes the words and photos.
  • Separate one-time costs (design and build) from monthly costs (hosting, updates, security, support) so quotes can be compared fairly.
  • Ask what you own at the end, what happens if you leave, and what is included each month before you say yes.
  • We don't quote prices in a blog post, because a number without context isn't useful. Ask for a written quote that lists everything.

Why there isn't one simple answer

You will see wildly different numbers if you ask around. That's normal. Two businesses can both say "I need a website" and mean completely different things.

A plumber in Camden County who needs a clear home page, a list of services, and a phone number that is easy to tap has a small job. A dental office with several providers, online booking, and a page for every treatment has a bigger one. A restaurant with an online menu that changes weekly has different needs again.

So rather than guessing a figure, it helps to know what moves the price up or down. Once you know that, any quote you get will make more sense. If you want to see how we build and price our own custom websites, you can start there.

What drives the price

How many pages you need

More pages means more design, more writing, and more testing. A site with a home page, services, about, and contact is quick to build. A site with a separate page for every service and every town you serve takes longer, but it can also give customers (and search engines) more to work with.

How custom the design is

Some sites start from a pre-made template and get adjusted. Others are designed from scratch around your brand. Neither is wrong. A template can be a sensible choice for a small business that needs to get online. Custom design takes more time and costs more, and it is worth it when you want the site to look and work like nothing else in your market.

What the site has to do

A simple brochure site shows who you are and how to reach you. Add any of these and the work grows:

  • Online booking or appointment requests
  • Quote forms that send to different people
  • Online payments or deposits
  • A members area or customer login
  • Connections to the software you already use

If you need something beyond a normal website, like a customer portal or a custom calculator, that moves into custom web apps, which is a different kind of project.

Who creates the content

Words, photos, and logos are part of a website. If you already have good photos and know what you want to say, the build is faster. If someone has to write every page, shoot photos, or design a logo, that is more work and it shows up in the quote. Real photos of your own crew, shop, or dining room almost always beat stock images, so it is worth planning for.

How fast you need it

Rushed projects and long, drawn-out ones both cost more in their own way. Our sites usually go live within a week because we keep the scope clear and the process simple. A clear scope is the biggest reason a project stays quick.

Search and AI visibility

A site that looks nice but that nobody can find is not doing its job. Some quotes include basic search setup, and some leave it out. Ask which it is. One-time costs versus monthly costs

This is where many owners get surprised. A website has two kinds of cost, and a good quote shows both clearly.

One-time costs

These are things you pay for once, at the start:

  • Design and build
  • Writing and photos, if they are part of the job
  • Setting up your domain (your web address, like yourbusiness.com) and email, if needed
  • Connecting forms, maps, and booking tools

Monthly or yearly costs

These keep the site running after launch:

  • Hosting, which is the computer that stores your site and serves it to visitors
  • Domain renewal, usually yearly, so you keep your web address
  • Security and backups, so a problem doesn't take your site down for good
  • Updates and fixes, because the software behind a site needs regular attention
  • Changes, like new hours, a new service, or a seasonal menu

Some of these may be bundled into one monthly fee. Some may be separate bills from different companies. Neither is automatically better, but you should know which you have. A cheap build with several surprise bills later can cost more than a clear all-in plan.

Questions to ask before you get a quote

Bring these to every conversation. The answers matter as much as the price.

  1. What exactly is included? Ask for the pages, features, and revisions listed in writing.
  2. What do I own when it's done? You should own your domain, your content, and your photos. Ask about the site itself too.
  3. What are the monthly costs, and what do they cover? Ask whether hosting, security, backups, and small edits are included.
  4. What happens if I want to leave? Can you take your site and domain with you? Is there a fee?
  5. Who makes changes, and how fast? A site that takes weeks to update a phone number is a problem.
  6. Is search setup included? Ask what is done so people can find you on Google and in AI tools.
  7. Will it work well on a phone? Most local customers look you up on their phone, often while standing in a parking lot deciding who to call.
  8. How long until it's live? Get a real date, not "soon."
  9. Who do I talk to when something breaks? A name and a way to reach them.

If someone can't answer these clearly, that tells you something. If a quote is only a single number with no list behind it, ask for the list.

How to compare quotes fairly

Put each quote side by side and fill in the same blanks: what is built, what you own, what you pay once, what you pay every month, and what is left out. Quotes only look comparable when they cover the same things.

Also be wary of a quote that sounds too good. Free or very low upfront prices often come with a long monthly contract, or with a site you don't own. That is not always bad, but you should know it going in.

Before you spend anything

You may already have a site that just needs work. If it gets visits but few calls, read website getting traffic but no calls. If people can't find you at all, start with why isn't my business showing up on Google. Your Google Business Profile is free and worth getting right either way.

Common questions

Can you tell me what a website will cost in this post?

No. A number without your pages, features, and needs behind it would be a guess. We would rather give you a written quote that lists exactly what is included. The free site check is a good first step.

Is a cheaper website always a worse one?

Not always. A smaller scope, a template, or content you supply yourself can lower the cost without hurting results. What matters is knowing what you are getting and what you own.

Do I really have to pay monthly?

A website needs hosting and regular updates and security attention, so someone is paying for them. You can pay a bundled monthly fee or handle the pieces separately. Ask which one a quote assumes.

Should I own my domain name?

Yes. Your domain is your business address online. Make sure it is registered in your name or your business name, not only the agency's.

How long does it take to build a small business website?

It depends on the scope and how quickly you can send content. Most of our sites go live within a week.
