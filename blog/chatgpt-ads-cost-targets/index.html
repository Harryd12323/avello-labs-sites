ChatGPT Ads are replacing bid caps with cost targets

ChatGPT ads cost targets are replacing the old bid caps, and local business owners should know what changed. Here is what it means for your website.

News · By Avello Labs · November 12, 2026 · 3 min read

In short

  • OpenAI is changing how advertisers control spending in ChatGPT Ads, swapping hard bid caps for average cost targets.
  • This only matters if you run, or plan to run, paid ads in ChatGPT, often through an agency or a tool.
  • Your website still decides whether a paid click turns into a call or a booking.
  • Getting found in ChatGPT's regular answers is a separate thing, and it is free.

What happened

Search Engine Journal reports that OpenAI is changing how bidding works in its ChatGPT Ads platform. Two manual limits, Max Bid and Max CPC (the most you will pay per click), are being phased out. In their place, advertisers set an average cost goal. For campaigns aimed at leads or sales, that is Target CPA, the average you want to pay for one result such as a purchase or a form fill. For campaigns aimed at visits, it is Target CPC, the average you want to pay for a click.

The new options became available in Ads Manager on November 9. The old caps stop applying to existing campaigns on January 1, 2027. Tools that connect through OpenAI's API, which is how software talks to the platform, see their old bid fields change meaning on November 18. A value sent in the old field will be treated as an average goal, not a limit.

An average is not a ceiling. Single clicks can cost more or less than your target. OpenAI also warns that a target set too low can hold back how much your ads run.

What it means for a local business

Most small businesses will never touch these settings. But if an agency or an app runs ads for you, this affects you.

Say you own an HVAC company in Burlington County and a marketing vendor sets up ChatGPT ads. Before, a bid cap meant no single click could go above a set amount. Now the system decides each bid itself, aiming for your average. Some clicks will cost more than you expected. That is normal, but it means you should watch the overall result, not each click.

It also means the page people land on matters more. Paying for a visit that ends on a slow or confusing page wastes the average you worked to set. If the visitor cannot find your phone number in a few seconds, the ad did nothing.

What to do about it

  1. Ask whoever runs your ads if you use ChatGPT Ads, and if they use Max Bid or Max CPC today.
  2. If you do, ask them to move to the new targets before January 1, 2027, so you choose the new goal instead of leaving it to chance.
  3. Have them base the target on what a lead or click has actually cost you recently, not a hopeful number.
  4. Check results as a whole: spend, calls, and form fills. Do not judge single clicks.
  5. Make sure the page your ad sends people to loads fast, works on a phone, and shows your phone number and booking button at the top. Our free site check can show you where it falls short.
  6. If you do not run ads, skip the bidding details. Work on being recommended in regular AI answers instead. That is what our AEO service is for, and we cover the basics in how to get recommended by ChatGPT.
