Ads in Google's AI answers are showing up, mostly in retail

Ads in Google AI answers are now common in some shopping categories. Here is what a new report found and what a local business should do about it.

News · By Harris, Avello Labs · November 23, 2026 · 3 min read

In short

  • A new report tracked paid ads inside Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode, and most were for clothing and electronics.
  • Google says existing Search, Shopping and Performance Max campaigns are eligible automatically, with no separate ad buy.
  • Local service businesses were not the focus, but AI answers are a growing place where customers see your competitors.
  • Your website and business listings still decide whether you get picked.

What happened

Search Engine Journal reports on a study from Adthena, a company that tracks advertising. It looked at paid ads shown inside Google's AI Overviews (the AI summary at the top of some results) and AI Mode (Google's chat-style search) in the U.S.

Across five holiday shopping categories, most of those ads were for apparel and consumer electronics. Travel, beauty and toys and games had far fewer.

Google's help page says advertisers do not buy these spots separately. Existing Search, Shopping and Performance Max campaigns can show in AI answers automatically. Advertisers can't target only AI Overviews or opt out of them alone, but they can use negative keywords (words that stop your ad showing for certain searches) and brand exclusions.

One caveat: the report shows where ads appeared, not clicks or sales, and it says nothing about local service businesses.

What it means for a local business

If you run a dentist office, a roofing company or a salon, this report is not about your category. You are unlikely to see a sudden flood of competitors' ads because of it.

Still, it shows the direction. AI answers are becoming a place where people compare options, with paid ads next to the answer. A homeowner in Burlington County who asks an AI tool for the best HVAC company may see a summary, some ads, and a few recommended names.

If you already run Google Ads, your campaigns may be showing in these spots without you choosing it. That is worth knowing.

If you don't run ads, your website matters even more. AI tools tend to name businesses whose websites clearly say what they do, where they work and how to reach them. A site with a clear page for each service gives them something to quote.

None of this needs a panic. The sensible move is to get your basics in order now.

What to do about it

  1. If you run Google Ads, ask whoever manages them whether your campaigns can appear in AI answers. Check your negative keywords and brand exclusions too.
  2. Make sure each service has its own page that states what you do and which towns you serve. Our guide on getting recommended by ChatGPT covers this.
  3. Keep your Google Business Profile accurate, with correct hours, services and photos.
  4. Check that AI tools can read your site with our AI crawler check.
  5. Don't change your ad budget based on one vendor's report.
