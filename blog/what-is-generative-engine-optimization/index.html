What is generative engine optimization (GEO)?

What is generative engine optimization? It's the work of making your business easy for AI tools to find, understand and mention when they write an answer. Here is what it means for a local business, and how it relates to SEO and AEO.

By Avello Labs · November 6, 2026 · 6 min read

In short

  • Generative engine optimization (GEO) means making your business easy for AI tools like ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's AI answers to find, understand and mention.
  • GEO, AEO and SEO overlap heavily. Most of the work is the same good basics.
  • Google says there are no extra technical requirements to appear in its AI features, and normal SEO fundamentals still apply.
  • For a local business, the practical steps are clear information, a complete Google Business Profile, and not blocking the tools that read your site.

What is generative engine optimization?

A "generative engine" is any tool that writes an answer for you instead of only listing links. ChatGPT is one. Perplexity is another. Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode are Google's versions. You ask a question, and the tool writes a short reply, often with a few sources it drew from.

Generative engine optimization, or GEO, is the practice of making your business a good source for those answers. The goal is simple. When someone asks "who is a good emergency plumber near Cherry Hill?", you want the tool to know you exist, understand what you do, and feel confident mentioning you.

The term is fairly new, and not everyone uses it the same way. Some agencies treat it as a brand-new field. Others say it is the same work as before with a new label. We sit closer to the second group, and we'll explain why below.

How GEO relates to SEO and AEO

These three terms confuse a lot of owners. Here is the short version.

  • SEO (search engine optimization) is helping your site show up in regular search results, like the list of links on Google. We cover why it matters in our SEO page.
  • AEO (answer engine optimization) is helping your business get picked when a tool gives a direct answer. That includes AI assistants and the answer boxes at the top of search. Our AEO page explains it in full.
  • GEO is very close to AEO. It puts the focus on AI tools that write their own answers, such as chatbots and AI summaries.

In practice, AEO and GEO are two names for nearly the same job. And both sit on top of SEO. An AI tool can only mention a business it can find, read and trust. Those are SEO jobs first.

Google has said something useful here. In its guidance for site owners, Google says there are no additional technical requirements to appear in AI Overviews and AI Mode. It also says specific optimization isn't required, and that existing SEO fundamentals continue to be worthwhile. So if someone tells you that you need a secret trick for Google's AI answers, be careful.

That doesn't mean nothing is different. Other AI tools, like ChatGPT and Perplexity, work differently from Google. How they pick sources is not fully public. Nobody outside those companies can tell you the exact formula, and anyone who claims to is guessing.

What actually helps your business show up in AI answers

Since the exact rules aren't public, we focus on things that are sensible, safe and useful to real customers too.

1. Say clearly what you do and where

AI tools read your pages like a very fast, very literal visitor. If your site says "quality service you can trust" and never says you are an HVAC company serving Burlington County, there is little to pick up.

Put the basics in plain words on your pages:

  • What you do (for example "furnace repair and replacement")
  • Where you work (towns and counties)
  • Your hours, phone number and address
  • Who you help and what a customer can expect

2. Answer real questions on your site

Customers ask the same questions all day. A dentist hears "do you take my insurance?" and "how long does a filling take?" Write those answers on your site in short, direct paragraphs. A question followed by a clear answer is easy for people and machines to read. Our post on getting recommended by ChatGPT goes deeper on this.

3. Keep your Google Business Profile complete

Your Google Business Profile is the listing that shows up on Google Maps and in local results. It holds your hours, services, photos and reviews. It is one of the clearest signals about a local business, so keep it accurate. We explain why in why your Google Business Profile matters.

4. Add structured data

Structured data, also called schema, is a bit of hidden code that labels your details for machines. It says "this is the business name, this is the phone number, these are the hours." It won't make any tool mention you, but it removes guesswork. Our free local business schema generator makes it easy.

5. Don't block the tools that read your site

Your site has a file called robots.txt. It tells automated visitors, called crawlers, what they may read. Sometimes a developer or a plugin blocks AI crawlers without the owner knowing.

It helps to know that some companies run separate crawlers for different jobs. OpenAI, for example, documents one crawler (GPTBot) used for training its models and another (OAI-SearchBot) used for search results in ChatGPT. You can allow one and block the other. Whether to allow either is your call. If you want to appear in ChatGPT search, blocking its search crawler works against you.

You can see what your site does with our AI crawler check, or read how to check if your site blocks AI crawlers.

6. Keep the site fast, secure and current

A slow, outdated or broken site is hard for anyone to rely on, people and machines alike. Fresh hours, current services and working pages matter. If you are unsure how often to refresh things, see how often should I update my website.

What GEO can't do

Be wary of big promises. No one can promise that ChatGPT, Perplexity or Google will mention your business. These tools change often, and their choices aren't published in full.

What you can do is make your business easy to find, easy to understand and easy to trust. That helps in regular search, in AI answers and with the customer who is reading your site right now. It's a good use of effort even if the labels keep changing.

Where to start

You don't need a new strategy. Start with a short list:

  1. Check that your site clearly states your services, towns, hours and phone number.
  2. Review your Google Business Profile for accuracy.
  3. Make sure you aren't blocking crawlers by accident.
  4. Add a short questions-and-answers section to your key pages.
  5. Look at how your site appears in search with our search preview tool.

Common questions

Is GEO different from AEO?

The two overlap almost completely. GEO usually refers to AI tools that write their own answers, and AEO covers any tool that gives a direct answer. The work is mostly the same, and you can read more on what AEO is.

Do I have to do GEO instead of SEO?

No. GEO builds on SEO. AI tools still need to find and understand your site, and Google says SEO fundamentals continue to matter for its AI features.

Can anyone promise my business will show up in ChatGPT?

No. The way these tools choose businesses isn't fully public and it changes. Be cautious of anyone who promises a result. You can only improve how clear and findable your business is.

Is GEO worth it for a small local business?

The basics are, because they also help regular customers and regular search. Clear pages, a good Google Business Profile and a working site are worth having either way. You don't need to buy anything fancy to start.
