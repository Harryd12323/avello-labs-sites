A website maintenance checklist for small business owners

A website maintenance checklist for small business owners keeps your site working, safe and accurate without taking over your week. Here is what to check every month, every year, and what to hand off.

By Harris, Avello Labs · November 16, 2026 · 6 min read

In short

  • Most website problems start small: a broken form, an old phone number, an expired certificate. A short monthly check catches them early.
  • Once a year, do a bigger review of content, ownership details, backups and whether the site still fits your business.
  • You can do this yourself in under an hour a month, hand it to a staff member, or have someone like us handle it.

Why a website needs regular maintenance

A website is not a brochure you print once. It is closer to a storefront. The lights need to work, the door needs to open, and the sign needs to say the right thing.

Things change around your site even when you touch nothing. Software gets updates. Plugins (add-on tools that give a site extra features, like a booking form) go out of date. A staff member leaves and their photo is still on the About page. A holiday passes and the banner still says "Open Thanksgiving week."

None of this feels urgent until a customer hits it. A plumber in Camden County whose contact form quietly stopped sending emails will not know until the phone is quiet for a few weeks. A checklist is how you find out first.

The monthly checklist

Set a recurring reminder for the same day each month. Open your site on your phone, because that is where most of your customers will see it. Then work through this list.

  1. Call your own number from the site. Tap the phone number on your homepage. Does it dial the right line? Does someone answer?
  2. Send a test message through every form. Use the contact form, the quote request and any booking form. Confirm the message actually arrives in your inbox, and check your spam folder too. Forms that fail silently are the most expensive problem on this list.
  3. Click through your main pages. Visit the homepage, services, about, contact and any page you send people to from ads or flyers. Look for broken links, missing images and pages that load slowly.
  4. Check hours, address and holiday notices. A salon that changed its Saturday hours and forgot the website sends people to a locked door. Remove old promotions and seasonal banners.
  5. Look for a browser warning. If your browser shows "Not secure" or a warning page, something is wrong with your site's security certificate. We explain that in what an SSL certificate is.
  6. Confirm updates are done. If your site runs on WordPress or a similar system, log in and see whether updates are waiting. Old software is a common way sites get hacked. If you are not sure how to do this safely, ask for help rather than clicking everything.
  7. Make sure backups exist. A backup is a saved copy of your whole site you can restore if something breaks. Ask your host or developer where the latest copy is and how recent it is.
  8. Read your reviews and update your photos. Add a recent job photo, a new menu item or a new team member. Fresh, true details help customers trust you.

Keep a simple log

Write down the date, what you checked and what you fixed. A shared note or spreadsheet works fine. If you hand this job to someone else, the log shows what has already been done.

The yearly checklist

Once a year, ideally in a slow month, go deeper. A restaurant might pick January. An HVAC company might pick late spring, before the busy summer season.

  • Renew and confirm ownership. Check that your domain name (your web address, like yourbusiness.com) is renewing, and that it is registered in your name, not a former employee's or an old web designer's. Make sure the renewal email goes to an inbox someone actually reads. Do the same for your hosting.
  • Review every page for accuracy. Read each page as a customer. Are the services still right? Are the staff bios current? Do your licenses, insurance and certifications still match?
  • Update your copyright year and legal pages. Check your privacy policy and any terms. If you collect information through forms, make sure the text still describes what you do.
  • Clean up users and logins. Remove accounts for anyone who no longer works with you. Change passwords, and turn on two step login (a code sent to your phone) where it is offered.
  • Test a restore. Ask whoever keeps your backups to confirm they can bring the site back. A backup nobody has tested is a hope, not a plan.
  • Look at your site on a few devices. Try an older phone, a tablet and a different browser. Layouts that looked fine two years ago can break.
  • Check how you look in search. Search for your business name and your main service plus your town. Our Google search preview tool shows how your page title and description may appear. Also review your Google Business Profile so the details match your site.
  • Check whether AI tools can read your site. Some sites accidentally tell AI crawlers (the programs AI tools use to read web pages) to stay out. Our AI crawler check looks at that file for you.
  • Ask whether the site still fits. If you have added services, changed your area or rebranded, the site may need more than a tune up. Our post on whether to fix or replace your site can help you decide.

How often to update content

Maintenance covers the technical side. Content is the other half. You do not need to publish constantly, but pages should not go stale. We cover this in more detail in how often you should update your website.

A good rule: whenever something real changes in your business, change the site that week. New service, new hours, new license, new photo. That is the whole rule.

Who should do the checklist

You have three realistic options.

  • Do it yourself. Fine for the monthly checks that only need your eyes and a phone. The technical items, like updates and backups, are where owners usually get stuck.
  • Hand it to a staff member. Print this list, assign one person and review the log together. Make sure they know who to call when something fails.
  • Have someone handle it. This is what our website care is for. We run through checks like these every month and fix problems, so you can focus on customers.

Common questions

How often should I do website maintenance?

Do a short check every month and a deeper review once a year. Security updates and backups should be handled on an ongoing basis, not saved for the yearly review.

What happens if I skip website maintenance?

Usually nothing dramatic at first. Then something small breaks, like a form, a certificate or a plugin, and you find out from a lost customer. Old software can also leave your site open to being hacked.

Can I do website maintenance myself?

Yes, for the visible checks like testing forms, phone numbers and page content. For updates, backups and security, ask someone experienced, because a wrong click can take the site down.

Do I need this if my site is brand new?

Yes. New sites still need updates, backups and accurate content. The checklist is shorter at first, but the habit matters.

What should I ask a web person who looks after my site?

Ask what they check each month, where backups are stored, who owns your domain and hosting, and how quickly they respond when something breaks. Ask to see a log of recent work.
