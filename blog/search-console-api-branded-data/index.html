Google adds branded and image search data to the Search Console API

Google has added branded search data to the Search Console API, which affects any reports built on it. Here is what that means for your business and what to do.

News · By Harris, Avello Labs · November 17, 2026 · 3 min read

In short

  • Google added three Search Console reporting features to its API, the connection that lets reporting tools pull your data automatically.
  • You can now see searches for your business name separately from other searches, and image-based searches have their own category.
  • You don't need to do anything technical. If an agency or tool reports on your site, ask whether it uses these.

What happened

Search Engine Journal reports that Google moved three features from the Search Console website into its API. Search Console is Google's free tool that shows how people find your site. An API is a way for other software to pull that data automatically, which is how many reporting dashboards work.

The first feature is a branded filter. It separates searches that include your business name from everything else. The second is data for image-based searches, such as Google Lens, Circle to Search on Android, and uploading a photo to Google. The third lets a report cover several countries or device types in a single request.

The branded data only starts on February 21, 2026, so there is no earlier split. Google also uses an automated process to sort searches, and it may occasionally get one wrong. Some small sites may see no branded data at all if they don't have enough search activity.

What it means for a local business

Most owners never touch the API. But the reports you see from an agency, a dashboard or a marketing tool may soon use it.

The branded split matters most. Say you run an HVAC company. Someone searching "Smith Heating and Air" already knows you. Someone searching "furnace repair near me" does not. Those are very different signals. If branded searches are going up, people are hearing about you. If only unbranded searches go up, you are being found by new customers.

Mixed together, those numbers can fool you. A busy month of referrals can look like better Google rankings, and the reverse can happen too.

Image search is a smaller piece for now. A salon or restaurant with good photos could be found this way, but the story says Google hasn't yet explained how far back that data goes.

What to do about it

  1. Open Search Console and look at the branded and non-branded view for your site. If it's empty, that is normal for a small site.
  2. Ask whoever sends you reports whether they separate branded from unbranded searches. If not, ask why.
  3. Don't compare branded numbers to anything before February 21, 2026. The data doesn't go back further.
  4. Make sure your business name, address and photos are consistent across your site and your Google Business Profile, since that helps both kinds of search.
