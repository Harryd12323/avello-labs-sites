How to get your products found by AI shopping tools

AI shopping tools need clear product details to recommend anything. Here is what an AI shopping webinar covered and what a local seller can do about it.

News · By Avello Labs · November 3, 2026 · 3 min read

In short

  • AI shopping tools can only recommend products they can read and understand.
  • Your product pages, your feed (the product data you send to Google) and your structured data (hidden labels that describe a page to machines) should all agree.
  • Measure shopping listings, AI mentions and paid results separately.
  • Start small with a few key products.

What happened

Search Engine Journal reports on a webinar from two people at seoClarity, an SEO software company. They covered how online stores can get products picked up by AI shopping tools in Google and ChatGPT.

They described four moves. Make product pages readable by bots. Describe products in the words shoppers use. Keep feeds, pages and structured data matching. Then measure what changed.

One warning stood out. Some pages show details to a person in a browser, but an automated visitor never sees them, often because the page relies on scripts to fill in the content. If a bot never receives your description, a better description won't help.

They also said a shopper's question often goes beyond color and material. People ask about warmth, durability or what a product is good for. Pages that answer those questions give AI more to work with.

What it means for a local business

Most of our readers are not running big online stores. But plenty sell a few products online: a boutique, a garden center, a bakery shipping gift boxes, a salon selling hair care.

Take a salon with a shampoo page that says only the brand and the size. A shopper asks an AI tool for a shampoo for dry, color-treated hair. The page never says who it suits, so the tool has little reason to mention it. A sentence about who the product is for, based on what it really does, fixes that.

The second lesson is consistency. If your price or product name differs between your site, your Google listing data and your feed, the systems get a muddled picture. The webinar also made the point that results come in different forms. A mention in an AI answer is not a sale. Track each one on its own.

Even if you sell no products, the same idea holds for services. Clear, readable, consistent pages are easier for AI tools to use. Our guide on getting recommended by ChatGPT covers that side.

What to do about it

  1. Pick a handful of your most important products. Don't start with everything.
  2. Check that bots can read them. Our AI crawler check is a quick place to begin.
  3. Add the answers shoppers ask for: who it's for, how it's used, how it holds up. Only say what is true of the product.
  4. Make sure the price, name and details match on your page, your feed and your structured data. Our schema generator can help with the labels.
  5. Write down where you stand today for shopping listings, AI mentions and any paid ads, so you can see change later.
  6. Change a few products first and leave others alone. That gives you something fair to compare against.
