AI shopping cards can show wrong details about your products. Here's what to check

AI shopping results now pull from product data across the web, and mistakes can cost you the sale. Here is what that means for a local business and how to check your own.

News · By Harris, Avello Labs · November 23, 2026 · 3 min read

In short

  • ChatGPT and Google now show product cards inside their answers, built from data spread around the web.
  • The common problems are a missing product, wrong details, or a buy link that goes to someone else.
  • Most of these trace back to information you control, like prices, stock and product names.
  • A simple check of your best sellers this month is a good first step.

What happened

Search Engine Journal published a sponsored guide from Writesonic, an AI tracking company. According to that guide, when someone asks ChatGPT or Google for a product recommendation, the answer often arrives as a product card. A card can include a photo, a price, a star rating, a short reason the item fits and a link to buy.

The problems it describes fall into a few groups. Your product may not appear at all, it may appear with the wrong details, or a reseller may get the click instead of you. The guide also notes that both platforms rely on product feeds. A feed is a file listing your products, prices and stock that you send to a platform. Because it is sponsored, the opinions are the sponsor's, and it promotes the company's own tool. The basic advice holds up without any tool.

What it means for a local business

Plenty of local businesses sell products online or list them for pickup. Think of a garden center, a bakery selling gift boxes, or a hardware store in Burlington County.

Picture a salon that sells a hair product on its own site. Its website says one price, a marketplace listing says another, and an old sale price sits on a third page. An AI tool has to pick one. It may pick the wrong one, or link to the marketplace instead of the salon.

The guide's main point is consistency. When your site, your listings and your product details disagree, AI guesses. Clear product information also helps tools know which item you mean. That is done with schema, which is hidden labeling in your page code that tells search engines what a page contains.

If you only offer services, the same idea applies to your hours, service names and prices.

What to do about it

  1. List your best sellers. Start with the few that matter most to your income.
  2. Ask ChatGPT and Google a few real buying questions, like "best gift basket near me." Note what shows up and where the link goes.
  3. Compare what you see with your own site. Fix any price, stock or name that doesn't match.
  4. Make sure each product page clearly states the name, size, color and use. Our schema generator can help with the labeling.
  5. Ask recent happy customers for fresh reviews, and update old product pages.
  6. Repeat the check now and then. AI answers change, so one look is only a snapshot.
