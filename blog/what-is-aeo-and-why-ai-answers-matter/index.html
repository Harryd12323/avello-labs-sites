What AEO is, and why AI answers matter for your business now

More people now ask an AI tool for a recommendation instead of searching and clicking through results. Here's what that means for a local business, and the few things worth doing about it.

By Avello Labs · October 20, 2026 · 4 min read

In short

  • AEO stands for answer engine optimization: making your business easy for AI tools to find and recommend.
  • AI answers often name only a handful of businesses, so being left out costs more than ranking a bit lower used to.
  • Most of the work is good SEO and accurate listings, plus a few checks that are specific to AI tools.

What AEO means

An answer engine is any tool that replies to a question with a written answer instead of a list of links. ChatGPT, Perplexity, Microsoft Copilot, Google's Gemini app, and the AI Overviews and AI Mode in Google Search all work this way.

AEO, short for answer engine optimization, is the work of making your business easy for those tools to find and trust. The goal is simple: when someone asks a question your business can answer, you have a fair chance of being named.

Why it matters now

Google now shows an AI-written summary, called an AI Overview, at the top of many search results, and it has added AI Mode, a chat-style way to search. ChatGPT and other assistants can search the web while they answer and name specific businesses, with links.

For a local business, the big change is how few names make it into an answer. In a page of ten results, being fifth still got you seen. In a written answer, you're either mentioned or you're not.

This doesn't mean regular search is going away. People still use Google Search and Google Maps every day, and the same work helps you there too. But it's worth making sure you're in good shape for both.

Where AI tools get their information

Nobody outside these companies knows exactly how each tool picks what to say, and they change often. What they share is that they build answers from what they can find on the web:

  • Search indexes. Google's AI features draw on Google Search, and Google says there are no extra technical requirements to appear in them beyond being indexed and eligible to show in search. Microsoft Copilot draws on Bing.
  • Your website, when their crawlers are allowed to read it.
  • Business listings like your Google Business Profile, Bing Places and Yelp.
  • What other people say about you: reviews, local news, directories and mentions on other sites.

When those sources agree with each other, a tool can describe you with confidence. When they don't, for example two different phone numbers or old opening hours, it may get your details wrong or leave you out.

What to do about it

None of this needs special software. Start at the top of this list and work down.

  1. Make sure AI bots can reach your site. Some sites block them by accident, often through a hosting or security setting. Our guide to checking for AI crawler blocks walks through it, or run the free AI crawler check.
  2. Say the basics clearly on your website. What you do, which towns you serve, your hours, how to book and, if you can share it, what things cost. Put this in normal text on the page, not only inside images or PDFs, so machines can read it.
  3. Answer the questions customers actually ask. Think about what people ask you on the phone, and answer those questions on your site in the words your customers use. A short FAQ page works well.
  4. Fill out your Google Business Profile. It's one of the main sources for local answers on Google. Our post on why your Business Profile matters has a checklist.
  5. Keep your details the same everywhere. Your business name, address, phone number and hours should match on your website, your Google profile, Yelp, Facebook and any industry directories.
  6. Add structured data to your site. This is a small block of code that labels your business name, location, hours and contact details for machines. Our free schema generator writes it for you.
  7. Earn reviews and local mentions. Ask happy customers for a review, and say yes when a local paper, business group or supplier wants to list or mention you.

How to see what AI tools say about you

The simplest test is to ask. Open ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity or Copilot and type the questions your customers would ask, like “best roofer in Cherry Hill” or “who can fix a leaking water heater near Cherry Hill”. Then ask directly about your business by name and check that the details are right.

Answers change from one day to the next and depend on the exact wording and where you are, so try a few versions and check again every month or two. If a tool gets something wrong, the fix is usually to correct the source it's likely reading, such as your website or your Business Profile.

An honest note. Nobody can guarantee that an AI tool will recommend your business. These tools make their own choices, and they change often. What you can control is whether your information is accurate, easy to read and consistent, which helps on Google and Maps as well as in AI answers.
