Wix vs Squarespace vs custom website: which fits a local business?

Wix vs Squarespace vs custom website is a fair question, and the honest answer is that each one fits some businesses. Here is how to tell which one fits yours.

By Harris, Avello Labs · November 25, 2026 · 6 min read

In short

  • A website builder like Wix or Squarespace is perfectly fine for many small local businesses, especially when you are just starting out.
  • A custom website makes more sense when you need specific features, tighter control over speed and search, or you are tired of working around a template.
  • Choose based on what the site must do for your business, and on who will look after it.
  • Check the current plan details on each company's own site before you buy. They change.

The short version

Wix and Squarespace are website builders. That means you pick a design template, drag your text and photos into place, and the company hosts the site for you. You do the building yourself, or someone does it on your behalf inside their system.

A custom website is built for your business from scratch, or from a lightweight starting point, instead of inside a builder's system. A person or studio builds it, hosts it, and looks after it.

Neither is better in every case. A builder is a good tool. A custom site is a different tool. The question is which job you need done.

When a website builder is perfectly fine

We build custom sites, and we will still tell you this: a builder is often the right call. Consider one if:

  • You are brand new and need something live while you figure out the business.
  • Your needs are simple. A few pages, your phone number, your hours, some photos, a contact form.
  • You enjoy tinkering and have the time to write and update the pages yourself.
  • You want to test the idea first before spending real money on anything bigger.

A solo dog groomer, a part-time photographer, or a new food truck can do very well with a builder site. What matters most for them is that the site exists, looks decent, and has correct contact details.

Builders also come with a lot built in. For example, TechRadar's review says Wix has built-in tools for events, bookings, restaurants and stores, with no third-party plugins needed. That is useful if you take bookings or run a small shop and want everything in one place. Squarespace is also a well-known option with polished templates, and many people like how its designs look out of the box.

Wix vs Squarespace: how they differ

We will not pretend there is a clear winner. Here is how people generally think about the two.

  • Wix is known for flexibility and a big range of add-ons. One review counts over 500 apps in the Wix App Market. More options can also mean more to sort through.
  • Squarespace is known for clean, design-led templates. Many people find it simpler because there are fewer choices to make.

Both change their plans, features and tools over time. We are deliberately not quoting plan details or costs here. Look at each company's own site for what is included today, and read what a plan covers before you commit.

If you are picking between the two, try both. Most builders let you start building before you pay. Pick the one where you can make the page you want without frustration.

When a custom website makes more sense

A builder starts to feel tight when the site has to do more than sit there. These are the signs we see with local businesses:

  1. You want the site to bring in calls, not just exist. A contractor, dentist or law firm usually needs pages that match what people in Camden, Burlington or Gloucester County actually search for, written for each service and town. That is a content and structure job. A builder can hold those pages, but someone still has to plan them.
  2. Speed matters to you. Slow pages lose visitors, especially on phones. Builders load their own tools on every page. A custom site can be kept lean. If you are curious how yours does, the free site check is a good place to start.
  3. You need something unusual. A quote request tool that routes by service, a patient intake flow, a client portal. That is where custom web apps come in.
  4. You are fighting the template. If you keep saying "I wish it could just do this," you have probably outgrown it.
  5. You want someone else to handle it. Many owners do not want to log in and fiddle. They want a site that is built, maintained and updated by someone who answers the phone.

A custom site is not magic. It will not bring in customers on its own. It gives you more control over how the site is built, how fast it loads, and how it is set up for search.

What to ask before you decide

Whichever way you lean, ask these questions:

  • Who will update the site? Be honest about whether it will be you, and whether you will actually do it.
  • Can I take my site with me if I leave? Ask the builder or the studio directly, and get the answer in writing. With a builder, find out what you can export and what stays behind. With a custom site, ask who owns the files and the domain.
  • Who owns my domain name? This is your web address. It should be registered in your name, whatever you choose.
  • What happens when something breaks? Builders have help centers. A studio should have a person.
  • Does it fit how customers find me? Look at your own search results. Does the site show your services, your service area and your reviews clearly?

If you already have a site and are not sure whether to replace it, this guide on fixing versus rebuilding walks through it. For the money side, our post on website costs covers what affects the price without making promises.

A simple way to choose

Ask yourself one thing: is my website a brochure, or is it part of how I get work?

If it is a brochure, a builder is likely enough. Pick one, fill it in carefully, keep your contact details current, and move on.

If it is part of how you get work, it deserves more planning. That might still be a builder, set up carefully. It might be a custom site. What matters is that someone is paying attention to it.

Whatever you choose, keep it looked after. Out-of-date hours, broken forms and old photos cost you trust. Ongoing website care is something we offer on the sites we build, but the same habits apply on any platform.

Common questions

Is Wix or Squarespace better for a small local business?

Neither wins in every case. Wix is known for its range of add-ons, and Squarespace for clean templates. Try both, and choose the one where you can build the pages you need without a fight.

Can a Wix or Squarespace site show up on Google?

Yes, builder sites can appear in search. Showing up depends on your content, your business details and your Google Business Profile, not only on the platform. We can't promise any ranking on any platform.

Is a custom website worth it for a small business?

It can be, when the site is a real part of how you get customers, or when you need features a template does not offer. If your needs are simple, a builder may serve you well.

Can I start on a builder and move to a custom site later?

Usually, yes. Your text, photos and domain can go with you, though the design itself will need rebuilding. Ask the platform what you can export before you start.

What should I look at before choosing any platform?

Check who will maintain it, who owns your domain, and what you can take with you if you leave. Then read the current plan details on the company's own site.
