A local SEO checklist for small businesses

Here is a local SEO checklist for small businesses, in the order we would do it. Work down the list, tick things off, and you will have covered the basics that matter most.

By Avello Labs · November 13, 2026 · 7 min read

In short

  • Start with your Google Business Profile, then fix your website, then reviews and listings.
  • Your name, address and phone number should read the same everywhere.
  • Most of this is free and you can do it yourself. Nobody can promise you a ranking, and we won't.

First, what local SEO means

SEO stands for search engine optimization. It means helping your business show up when people search. Local SEO is the version for businesses that serve a place, like a roofer in Gloucester County, a salon in Burlington County or a dentist near Camden.

If you want the bigger picture of why this matters, our SEO page covers it. This post is the to-do list. Go in order. The early items matter most. If you would like a quick read on where your site stands before you start, our free site check is a good first stop.

Step 1: Claim and complete your Google Business Profile

Your Google Business Profile is the free listing that appears in Google Maps and in the box of local results. It is the single best place to start. We wrote more on why in why your Google Business Profile matters.

Google says to complete your business details in Google Business Profile to help your business match searches. So fill in everything.

  • Claim the profile and finish verification, which is Google's way of confirming you really run the business.
  • Pick the main category that best describes what you do. A plumber should pick plumber, not "home services".
  • Add secondary categories only if they are true.
  • Enter your hours, including holiday hours when they change.
  • List your services. A med spa can list each treatment. An HVAC company can list repair, installation and maintenance.
  • Add your service area if you travel to customers.
  • Upload real photos of your work, your space and your staff.
  • Write a short, honest description of what you do and who you serve.

Use your real business name. Do not stuff extra keywords into it. That can get a profile suspended.

Step 2: Make your name, address and phone match everywhere

Search engines compare what different sites say about you. If one place says "Main St." and another says "Main Street," or you have an old phone number floating around, it creates doubt.

Pick one exact version of your name, address and phone number. Then check these places:

  1. Your website footer and contact page
  2. Your Google Business Profile
  3. Facebook and Instagram
  4. Apple Maps and Bing Places
  5. Yelp and any industry directories, like a bar association page for a law firm or a trade group for contractors
  6. Your chamber of commerce listing

Fix anything that does not match. Close duplicate listings if you find them. Be careful with that one, and follow each site's own instructions.

Step 3: Check your website basics

Your website backs up what your profile says. Here is what to look at.

Make sure each service has its own page

One page that says "Services" with a list is weak. A page for "Water heater replacement" is much stronger. Give each main service its own page. Explain what you do, who it is for, and where you do it.

Mention your town and area naturally

Say where you work. "We serve homeowners across Camden County" helps both people and search engines. Do not paste a list of fifty towns at the bottom of the page. Mention the places you really serve.

Write good page titles

The page title is the blue link people see in search results. Keep it clear: what you do and where. Our search preview tool shows how your title and description may look in Google.

Put your phone number and a clear next step on every page

If someone lands on your site and can't find how to call you, the best ranking in the world will not help. If you get visits but no calls, read website getting traffic but no calls.

Make sure it works on a phone

Most local searches happen on phones. Open your site on yours. Can you read the text? Can you tap the call button? Does it load without a long wait?

Check that your site is secure

Your address should start with https, not http. That comes from an SSL certificate. Here is what an SSL certificate is if that is new to you.

Step 4: Add local business schema

Schema is a block of hidden code on your page. It labels your business details, like name, address, hours and services, so software can read them without guessing. You do not have to write it by hand. Our free local business schema generator builds it for you, and you can paste the result into your site or hand it to whoever manages it.

Schema does not guarantee anything. It just removes guesswork about who you are.

Step 5: Ask for reviews, and answer them

Reviews matter. Google says more reviews and positive ratings can improve your business's local ranking. They also help the person deciding between you and the next business.

  • Ask every happy customer, right after the job is done. A text with a direct link works well.
  • Make it easy. Don't ask people to hunt for your listing.
  • Reply to every review, good or bad. Keep replies short, polite and specific.
  • Never pay for reviews or write your own. It breaks the rules and customers can tell.
  • Don't offer discounts in exchange for reviews.

For a dentist or law firm, be careful about what you say in replies. Never confirm that someone was a patient or client. Keep it general.

Step 6: Keep it fresh

Local SEO is not a one-time job. Hours change. Services change. New photos go stale.

  • Post a profile update when you have news, an offer or a finished project.
  • Add new photos regularly.
  • Review your website now and then for old information.
  • Answer questions people ask on your profile.

We cover this in how often you should update your website. If you would rather not think about it, that is what website care is for.

Step 7: Check that AI tools can find you

More people now ask tools like ChatGPT and Google's AI answers for a recommendation. Those tools need to read your site. One thing to check is that your robots.txt file, a small file that tells automated visitors what they may read, is not blocking them by mistake. Try our AI crawler check, or read how to check if your site blocks AI crawlers. For the bigger picture, see what AEO is.

Nobody outside these companies knows exactly how they pick businesses to mention, so treat this as good housekeeping, not a promise.

A simple order to work in

If the list feels long, do this first:

  1. Finish your Google Business Profile.
  2. Fix your name, address and phone number everywhere.
  3. Give each main service its own page.
  4. Start asking for reviews.
  5. Add schema.

Still not appearing in searches? See why isn't my business showing up on Google. If your site is too far gone, do I need a new website or can mine be fixed can help you decide.

Common questions

Can I do local SEO myself?

Yes. Most of this list is free and needs no special skills, just time and care. The parts that take longer are website pages and schema, and that is where many owners ask for help.

How long until I see results?

Nobody can say, and anyone who gives you a date is guessing. Changes can take time to show up, and competition in your area plays a part.

Do I need a new website for local SEO?

Not always. Many sites can be fixed. If the site is slow, hard to use on a phone, or can't be edited, a rebuild may make more sense.

Which step matters most?

Your Google Business Profile. For most local businesses, it is the first thing people see, so start there.

Does this work for service-area businesses with no storefront?

Yes. Set your service area on your profile, and build pages on your site for the places and services you cover.
