Why isn't my business showing up on Google?

If you're asking why isn't my business showing up on Google, the cause is usually one of a few fixable things. Here they are in order of likelihood, with a way to check each one.

By Avello Labs · October 25, 2026 · 7 min read

In short

  • Most often, the business has no claimed Google Business Profile, or the profile isn't verified.
  • Next most common: Google can't add your website pages to its index (its big list of pages), or something tells it to stay away.
  • A brand new site, or one with very little on it, can simply be too thin for Google to show yet.
  • Every check below is free and takes a few minutes. You don't need to be technical.

Start with what "not showing up" means

Owners use this phrase for three different problems. Work out which one is yours, because the fix is different.

  • You search your business name and don't appear. This is the most serious one. It points to an indexing or blocking problem.
  • You appear for your name but not for "plumber near me" or similar. This is a ranking problem, not a visibility problem. Your site is known to Google, but others look like better answers.
  • Your website shows up but your map listing doesn't. This is almost always a Business Profile problem.

Try each search in a private browser window, so your own history doesn't skew what you see. Then go through the causes below in order.

Cause 1: No Business Profile, or one that isn't verified

For local businesses this is the most common reason. The map results you see for searches like "HVAC company near me" come from Business Profiles, not from websites. A great website won't put you on the map if you have no profile.

How to check:

  1. Search your exact business name plus your town.
  2. Look for a box on the right of the results (or at the top on a phone) with your name, hours and reviews.
  3. If you see it, look for an option that says you own it or can claim it. If it's offered, nobody has verified the listing yet.
  4. If there's nothing at all, you probably need to create one.

Verification is the step many owners skip. Google asks you to prove the business is yours before you can manage it. Until then, your details can be wrong, missing or edited by someone else.

Also check the basics: name, address (or service area, if you travel to customers, like most contractors do), phone number, hours, and a clear main category. A wrong category is a quiet way to stay hidden. A dentist listed as a generic "health" business won't match what patients search.

We wrote more on this in why your Google Business Profile matters.

Cause 2: A page Google can't or won't index

Google keeps a huge list of web pages, called an index. If your page isn't in it, it can't appear in search results. Not being in the index is different from ranking badly. It means you aren't in the running at all.

The quick check: type site:yourwebsite.com into Google, using your own address. If pages from your site appear, Google knows about them. If you get nothing, something is wrong.

The better check: use Google Search Console. It's a free tool from Google that shows how it sees your site. Its URL Inspection tool lets you paste in a page address. According to Search Engine Land's guide, the most common use of this tool is to check whether a page is indexed and eligible to appear in Google Search. You'll get a clear yes or no, and often a reason if the answer is no.

Your web designer or whoever set up your domain can add you to Search Console. If nobody can tell you who has access, that's worth finding out anyway.

Common reasons a page isn't indexed:

  • The site is so new that Google hasn't visited yet.
  • The page is hidden behind a login or a form.
  • The page was set to "noindex" (more on that next).
  • Google found the page but judged it a copy of another page, or not useful enough.

Cause 3: Blocked crawlers

Google uses a program called a crawler (Googlebot) to visit pages. Two small settings can tell it to stay away.

  • robots.txt is a plain text file at the root of your site. It tells crawlers which areas they may visit.
  • A noindex tag is a note inside a page that asks search engines not to list it.

Both are useful when set on purpose. The trouble is when they're left on by mistake. A common story: a designer builds a new site on a hidden test version, switches on "discourage search engines," and then the site goes live with that setting still on. The site looks perfect and is invisible.

How to check:

  1. Visit yourwebsite.com/robots.txt in your browser. You'll see a short file. A line that blocks everything (a lone slash after a "Disallow" line) is a red flag.
  2. If your site runs on WordPress, open Settings, then Reading, and make sure the box about discouraging search engines is not ticked.
  3. Run a page through Search Console's URL Inspection tool. It will tell you if the page is blocked or marked noindex.

Cause 4: A new site, or a thin one

If your site is new, patience is part of the answer. Google has to find it, visit it and decide where it fits. Nobody outside Google knows exactly how long that takes, and we won't guess.

You can help it along:

  • Make sure your site is in Search Console.
  • Link to your website from your Business Profile and social pages.
  • Make sure every service has its own page.

A thin site is the other half of this. Picture an electrician with one home page saying "Quality work, call us." Google has little to go on. It can't tell what you do, where you do it, or why you're better than the next electrician.

Good pages for a local business say:

  • Each service you offer, on its own page.
  • Each area you serve, such as Camden County, Burlington County or Gloucester County, if you really work there.
  • Real details: what a job involves, how you work, what people ask you.
  • Your name, address and phone number, written the same way everywhere.

What if everything checks out?

Then you're probably in the ranking problem, not the visibility problem. Google knows you exist, but other businesses look stronger for the searches that matter. That's what local SEO is for: clearer pages, a well kept profile, steady reviews and a site that loads fast on a phone.

Common questions

How do I know if Google has my website at all?

Type site: followed by your web address into Google. If pages appear, Google has them. For a clearer answer, paste a page address into the URL Inspection tool in Google Search Console.

I have a website. Why don't I show on the map?

The map results come from Business Profiles, not websites. You likely need to claim and verify a profile, or fix the name, category or address on the one you have.

Can I block Google by accident?

Yes. A leftover robots.txt rule or a noindex setting from when the site was being built can keep it out of search. It's one of the first things to check.

How long should I wait before worrying about a new site?

Google's timing isn't public, so there's no honest number. If you can't find your site by name after you've confirmed it isn't blocked and is in Search Console, ask someone to look into it.
