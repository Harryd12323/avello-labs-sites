AI shopping agents often fall for fake deals, and what that means for your site

A new test shows AI shopping agents often trust a listing's ratings over common sense. Here is what that means for your website.

News · By Harris, Avello Labs · November 26, 2026 · 3 min read

In short

  • A researcher set nine AI models loose on a shopping task with made-up listings, and most of them bought an obvious fake deal at least some of the time.
  • Ratings and review volume drove the choices more than price, awards or refunds.
  • A local business can't fix this for shoppers, but it can make its real ratings, details and policies easy for AI tools to read.

What happened

Search Engine Journal reports on a test where a researcher had AI models from Anthropic, OpenAI and Google go shopping for Christmas presents. Each present came with a shelf of made-up listings. One was a fake: a perfect rating from a huge number of reviews, at a fraction of the normal price.

Eight of the nine models bought the fake at least some of the time. The top Claude model never did. The models often wrote that the deal looked suspicious, then bought it anyway. They could ask the researcher a question before buying, but almost never asked about the deal they doubted.

Other findings: the fake did better when it was listed first, and when the AI was asked for the "best value." With the fake removed, the best-rated listing won most of the time. A real lower price, an award or a full refund mattered far less. A hidden note telling AIs to pick one listing barely worked.

The researcher tested the models directly, not the consumer apps, which may add their own checks.

What it means for a local business

Shoppers are starting to hand small jobs to AI tools. Today that is mostly gift shopping. The same habits could show up when an AI helps someone pick a plumber or a salon.

The main lesson is that these tools lean hard on ratings and review signals. Picture an HVAC company with a solid rating and honest reviews. An AI comparing it to a rival with an odd, too-perfect profile might not weigh the difference carefully. You can't control that. Nobody can fix it by chasing a perfect score either, and we wouldn't try.

What you can control is how clear and consistent your information is. Your hours, services, service area, warranties and reviews should say the same thing on your site and your Google profile. Clear, honest details give an AI more to work with than a star count alone. This is part of what we call AEO, or answer engine optimization: getting recommended by AI tools like ChatGPT. Our guide to getting recommended by ChatGPT covers the basics.

What to do about it

  1. Check that your name, address, phone number, hours and services match everywhere they appear. Start with your site and your Google Business Profile.
  2. Put your real policies in plain view on your site: warranties, refunds, service areas and how quoting works.
  3. Keep earning honest reviews, and reply to them. Don't chase a perfect score.
  4. Make sure AI tools are allowed to read your site. Our AI crawler check shows whether they are blocked.
  5. If you ever let an AI pick something for you, look at what else was available before you pay.
  6. Run our free site check to see how your site reads to search and AI tools. Our AEO service can help with the rest.
