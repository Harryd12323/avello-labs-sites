Google is turning off Search in schools that say no to its AI

Google Search is being switched off for student accounts at schools that decline Google's AI. Here is what that news means for a local business website, and what to do.

News · By Avello Labs · November 4, 2026 · 3 min read

In short

  • Google is turning off Search, along with its Gemini AI tools, for K-12 student accounts unless a school administrator turns them on.
  • Schools can't keep Search and switch off the AI features. It is one choice or the other.
  • This only affects school-managed student accounts. It doesn't change how the public finds your business.
  • Good basics still matter: a clear site, an accurate Google Business Profile and pages that work for both search and AI answers.

What happened

The Verge reports that Google is changing how it handles accounts for students in kindergarten through 12th grade. Gemini, Google's AI assistant, and Search will both be off for those accounts unless a school administrator deliberately turns them on.

The catch is that a school can't keep Search while refusing the AI. Google's education vice president told the New York Times that Search and AI are deeply connected. A school that disables the AI features loses Search too, and students will see a message from the school saying it isn't available.

Google says this gives schools more choice, and that each service can be turned on or off on its own. Several public officials have criticized the move. Google also said it will test a feature in some US schools that helps students work through problems instead of just getting answers.

What it means for a local business

For most owners, nothing changes today. This is about accounts that schools manage for students. It isn't about how adults or families find a plumber, a dentist or a pizza place.

Still, it's a useful reminder. Google is putting AI more deeply into Search itself, so the two are becoming harder to separate. People now find businesses through the regular results, through AI answers and through Google Maps.

Think of a tutoring center in Camden County. Parents search for it, and so do older students. If your business serves families, some of your audience may be using a school-run account that behaves differently. That makes it worth having a site that doesn't depend on one way of being found.

We wouldn't read more into this than that. The story doesn't say what comes next, and neither will we.

What to do about it

  1. Look at your own site the way a customer would. Check that your services, hours, address and phone number are easy to find on every page. Our free site check can show you what to fix first.
  2. Make sure your Google Business Profile is accurate. It helps people find you whether they use Search, Maps or AI answers. See why it matters.
  3. Write pages that answer real questions in plain sentences. That helps regular search and AI tools. Our guide to getting recommended by ChatGPT explains how.
  4. Check that you aren't blocking AI tools by accident. Our AI crawler check takes a minute.
  5. Keep other ways to reach you alive, such as a mailing list, referrals and your social pages. Don't rely on one door.
