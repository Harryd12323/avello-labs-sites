Your website isn't bringing in calls? Here's what's usually going on.

Maybe the site is old, maybe it's slow, or maybe people visit and leave without getting in touch. Either way, a website that doesn't lead to calls is costing you work you never hear about.

Why this happens

People who visit a small business website usually want to know two things: whether you can help, and how to reach you. When a site gets in the way of that, they go back and pick someone else.

  • It's hard to call from a phone. The number is buried, or tapping it doesn't start a call.
  • It's slow, especially on phones. Google measures how real visitors experience loading and responsiveness with a set of metrics called Core Web Vitals. A slow site loses people before they see anything.
  • It doesn't say clearly what you do and where. Visitors should know in a few seconds whether you're the right fit.
  • It looks out of date. Old photos, last year's hours or a copyright from years ago make people wonder if you're still in business.
  • The contact form quietly stopped working. Forms can break without any error, so messages never arrive.

Something you may not have considered

More visitors won't fix a site that doesn't turn visitors into calls. Many businesses spend money getting more people to their site, when the real leak is what happens after they arrive.

And a site can stop working without anyone noticing. Try your own site on your phone today: tap the phone number, send yourself a message through the form, and check that your hours are right. Our free site check looks at the technical side for you.

Before you decide

  1. Fix it, or start over? If the site is fairly new and mostly works, targeted fixes may be enough. If it's hard to change or very out of date, a rebuild can cost less in the long run. Our post on whether to fix or replace a website walks through it.
  2. Pick the one thing you want visitors to do. Call, book or request a quote. A site that asks for one clear action usually works better than one that asks for five.
  3. Decide who keeps it working after launch. Even a great site drifts out of date. Decide whether that's you, someone on staff, or a service like website care.
