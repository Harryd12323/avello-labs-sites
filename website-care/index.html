Ongoing site health: what it is and why it matters

A website isn't a sign you put up once. It runs on software, depends on services that renew and change, and describes a business that keeps changing. Ongoing care keeps it working, accurate and trusted.

In short

  • Websites tend to break quietly: a form stops sending, a page goes missing, a certificate expires.
  • Most problems are cheap to fix early and expensive to discover late.
  • Care covers the technical side (updates, security, speed, backups) and the content side (hours, services, prices, photos).
  • It also keeps up the foundations that SEO and AEO depend on.

What goes wrong when nobody's watching

  • Contact forms stop working, often without any error, so messages simply never arrive.
  • Software and plugins fall out of date and become security risks.
  • The security certificate or domain name lapses, and browsers warn visitors away.
  • Pages break, or links point nowhere, after small changes.
  • The site slows down as images and add-ons pile up.
  • Information goes stale: old hours, discontinued services, last year's prices, a holiday closure still showing in March.

None of these announce themselves. You usually find out when a customer mentions it, or when they don't call at all.

What ongoing care includes

Technical health

  • Software, theme and plugin updates
  • Uptime monitoring, so outages are caught fast
  • Regular backups that can be restored
  • Speed, especially on phones
  • Domain and certificate renewals

Search and content health

  • Test messages through every form
  • Google Search Console errors and warnings
  • Broken links and missing pages
  • Up-to-date hours, services, prices and photos
  • Accessibility for every visitor

On speed: Google measures how real visitors experience loading, responsiveness and layout stability with a set of metrics called Core Web Vitals. They're a useful, concrete way to tell whether a site feels fast or frustrating.

Why it matters for a small business

Your website is often the first impression a customer gets. A broken form or outdated hours costs you customers you never hear about. Search engines and AI tools favor sites that are fast, secure and accurate, so neglect slowly wears away your visibility too.

And repairing a hacked or broken site after the fact usually takes far more time and money than keeping it healthy in the first place.

What a month of care can look like

  • Check that the site is up, fast and secure
  • Apply updates and confirm nothing broke
  • Send a test message through every form
  • Review Search Console for new errors
  • Update anything that changed: hours, services, offers, photos
  • Note anything that needs your decision, in plain English

Site care goes hand in hand with SEO and AEO: a slow, broken or outdated site is hard to rank or recommend, however good everything else is.

Common questions

My site is new. Does it still need care?

Yes. New sites still need updates, monitoring and backups, and the first few months are when you'll most want to adjust your content based on what customers ask.

My site is on Wix or Squarespace. Isn't that handled for me?

Those platforms handle hosting, security and software updates, which is a real advantage. They don't keep your content current, check that your forms reach you, fix SEO issues, or watch how you show up in search. That part is still up to you.

What happens if my site goes down?

With monitoring in place, you find out quickly instead of from a customer. The cause can be tracked down and fixed, and recent backups mean the site can be restored if needed.

Do I need to give someone access to my website?

Usually, yes, at the level needed to make updates. We recommend that you always own your domain, hosting and website accounts in your own name, and add anyone helping you as a separate user, so you're never locked out.

How is this different from SEO?

Site care keeps the website itself healthy. SEO and AEO work on how you're found. They depend on each other, because a slow, broken or outdated site is hard to rank.
