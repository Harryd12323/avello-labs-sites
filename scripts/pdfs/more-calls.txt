# A website that turns visitors into calls
Subtitle: What a local business site needs, whether you're starting fresh or fixing what you have

A good website for a local business has one job: help the right people pick up the phone, book or visit. This guide covers what that takes, including sites that mainly show off your work.

## 1. The first screen
The top of your home page should answer three questions without any scrolling:
- What do you do?
- Where do you do it?
- How do I get in touch right now?
Put your phone number at the top of every page, and make it tap to call on phones.

## 2. One clear next step
- Pick the action you want most (call, book, or ask for a quote) and make it the main button on every page.
- Use button words that say what happens, like “Call for a free quote”, instead of “Submit” or “Learn more”.
- Keep forms short. Name, email or phone, and one line about what they need is usually enough.

## 3. Fast on phones
- Many customers will find you on a phone. Open your site on yours, on mobile data, and see how long it takes.
- Large photos are a common cause of a slow site. Resize them before you upload.
- Google's free PageSpeed Insights tool shows what's slowing a page down.

## 4. Showing off your work (galleries and portfolios)
If your work sells itself, like building, landscaping, design, art or hair, your gallery is your sales pitch.
- Lead with your best work, not all of it.
- Give each project a short caption: what it was, where, and what the customer wanted.
- Put a “Want something like this?” button next to the gallery, not just at the bottom of the page.
- Use real photos. Customers can spot stock images.

## 5. Trust at the moment of decision
- Show real reviews near your main buttons, with names if customers agree.
- Add your licences, insurance, guarantees you actually offer, and how long you've been in business.
- Add photos of you and your team. People like to know who they're hiring.

## 6. Selling products
- Every product needs clear photos, a price, and the details customers ask about.
- Say how delivery or pickup works and what it costs before checkout, not during it.

## Starting from scratch?
You don't need a big site. A home page, one page per main service, a gallery if it fits your trade, and a contact page will cover most local businesses. Get those right first, then add more.
