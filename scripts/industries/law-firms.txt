## What gets in the way
- **One page covers every practice area.** Someone searching for a family lawyer in Cherry Hill lands on a page that also talks about real estate and wills, and leaves.
- **The site talks about the firm, not the client's problem.** A worried reader wants to know if you handle what they're facing and what happens next.
- **It's hard to reach someone after hours.** The phone number is buried and the form gives no hint of when they'll hear back.
- **Your attorneys' bios are thin or out of date.** People want to know who they'd be working with.
- **AI tools describe the firm wrongly, or not at all.** More people ask ChatGPT or Google's AI answers who to call.

## What we do about it
We give each practice area its own clear page, in plain words, that says who it's for and what the first step is. That's what Google and AI tools need to match you to the right search.

We make getting in touch easy on a phone, and we say plainly what happens after someone reaches out.

We keep attorney bios, office locations and hours current, and make sure your Google Business Profile matches the site.

Every month we look after the site and check how you show up in South Jersey and Philadelphia searches, so it keeps working while you're in court.

## Advertising rules and your site
Lawyer advertising is regulated by the state, and New Jersey and Pennsylvania each have their own rules. We keep what we write factual. No made up results, no “best lawyer” claims, no testimonials you haven't approved.

You review every page before it goes live, because you know the rules for your practice. We're not giving legal or ethics advice. If you're unsure about a line, check with your own advisers and we'll change it.

## A month on the Growth plan
We post to your Google Business Profile, add one page or post a month about a question clients often ask, and check how the firm shows up on Google and in AI answers. You get a short report and sign off on anything new before it's published.

## Common questions
Q: Can you write pages about our practice areas?
A: We draft them in plain words and you check every one. We don't publish anything about the law or your results without your OK.
Q: Do you put case results on the site?
A: Only if you want them and you've confirmed they're allowed under your state's rules. We never write results for you.
Q: We have offices in New Jersey and Pennsylvania. Can you handle both?
A: Yes. Each office gets its own details and its own place on Google, so people near either one can find you.
