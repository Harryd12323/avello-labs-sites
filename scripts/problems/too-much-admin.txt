## Why this happens
Most businesses don't plan to run on spreadsheets and copy and paste. It builds up one workaround at a time.

- **Information lives in too many places.** Spreadsheets, email, paper, texts and a few different apps, none of which talk to each other.
- **The same details get typed more than once.** A customer's name goes into the quote, then the invoice, then the schedule.
- **Off-the-shelf software almost fits.** It does most of the job, so people fill the gaps by hand.
- **Reports get built from scratch.** Someone pulls numbers together every week because no system does it for them.

## Something you may not have considered
You may not need custom software at all. Often the tools you already pay for can do more than you're using: a setting, a built-in report or a connection between two apps can remove a whole step.

When nothing off the shelf fits, a small custom tool can be simpler than people expect. For example, we built a Windows desktop app for a field sales company that turns their GPS data exports into weekly tech logs, a job that had been done by hand every week.

## Before you decide
1. **Write down the steps.** List exactly what happens, in order, and who does each part. It's the fastest way to see which step is the real time sink.
2. **Check what you already pay for.** Look for features, reports or connections you haven't switched on before buying anything new.
3. **Think about who will use it, and on what.** A tool for the office computer is different from one people use on their phones in the field. And decide who looks after it once it's built.
