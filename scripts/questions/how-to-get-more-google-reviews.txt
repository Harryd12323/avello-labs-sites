## The short answer
Ask. Most people who had a good experience are happy to leave a review, they just don't think of it. Make it easy with a direct link, and ask at the moment they're pleased: when the job's done, or when they thank you.

Your Google Business Profile has a share link for reviews. Put it in a text, an email or on a card.

## What Google doesn't allow
Google's review policies don't allow offering discounts or gifts in exchange for reviews. They also don't allow asking only the customers you expect to be happy, which is often called review gating. Ask everyone the same way. A few honest mixed reviews look more real than a wall of perfect ones anyway.

## Make it a habit
- Ask at the same point every time, so it doesn't depend on remembering.
- Reply to every review, good and bad. Short and polite is fine.
- Use what people say. If customers keep mentioning a service, make sure your website has a page about it.

## Common questions
Q: Can I ask friends and family to leave reviews?
A: Google's policies say reviews should reflect a real experience with the business. Reviews from people who aren't real customers can be removed.
Q: What do I do about a bad review?
A: Reply calmly, thank them, and offer to sort it out offline. Other customers read your reply too. If a review breaks Google's policies, you can report it.
Q: Do reviews help me rank?
A: Google says prominence, which affects local ranking, is partly based on review count and ratings. Reviews also help people decide to call.
